How the Instagram algorithm actually works

There isn't one algorithm. Feed, Reels, Explore and Stories each rank posts their own way. Here's what each one looks at, in Instagram's own words, and what that means for your next post.

Check a post nowRead Instagram's explanation

3 signals

Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, names watch time, sends per reach and likes per reach as the most important signals for reach.Buffer

4 algorithms

Feed, Stories, Explore and Reels each use their own ranking, and Search has its own rules too.Instagram

Original wins

Instagram ranks original content above reposts, and can show reels less if they were already posted on Instagram.Instagram

What makes Instagram show a post less

  • Low resolution
  • Watermarks from other apps
  • Muted (no sound)
  • Borders around the video
  • Mostly text
  • Already posted on Instagram

These are the reasons Instagram itself lists for making a reel less visible.Instagram

Not recommended is not the same as removed

Posts that break Instagram's Recommendation Guidelines stay on your profile but aren't shown to new people in Explore or Reels.

Accounts that keep breaking those guidelines can have none of their posts recommended for a while. Posts that break the Community Standards are removed.

Check Settings, then Account Status to see if your account can be recommended, and appeal if Instagram got it wrong.Instagram

Four things people get wrong

"There's one Instagram algorithm."

No. Instagram says each part of the app (Feed, Stories, Explore, Reels and Search) uses its own algorithm, tailored to how people use it.Instagram

"Use 30 hashtags."

Instagram caps hashtags at 5 per post since December 2025. Pick specific ones.Business Today

"I've been shadowbanned."

Instagram points creators to Account Status, which shows whether your account and content can be recommended, including in Search, and lets you appeal.Instagram

"Testing new ideas will hurt my account."

Trial reels are shown only to people who don't follow you, and stay off your grid until you choose to share them.TechCrunch
